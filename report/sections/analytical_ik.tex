\section{Aalytical Inverse Kinematics}
\label{sec:analytical-ik}

Derivation:

We split the problem in two, first solving the position of the wrist-center, which reduces the inverse kinematics to those of a 3R robot.
After having the position of the wrist, we add the three remaining DOFs solving for its orientation.

1. Wrist center position:

The wrist position can be easily found from the desiired end effector pose $T_d = \begin{pmatrix}
  R_d & p_d \\
  0^T & 1 \\
\end{pmatrix}$:

$$ p_w = p_d - d_6 R_d e_x = \begin{pmatrix}
  x_w \\
  y_w \\
  z_w
\end{pmatrix} $$ 

To make computations easier, we can undo the waist roation with $R_z(-q_1)=\begin{pmatrix}
  c_1 & s_1 & 0 \\
  -s_1 & c_1 & 0 \\
  0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}$:

$$ u = R_z(-q_1)p_w = \begin{pmatrix}
  c_1x_w + s_1y_w \\
  -s_1x_w+c_1y_w \\
  z_w
\end{pmatrix} $$ 

In the waist adjusted coordinates, the shoulder (joint 2) is at $s = \begin{pmatrix}
    a_1 \\
    0 \\
    d_1
\end{pmatrix}$. The vector from the shoulder to the wrist center in the waist adjusted
coordinates is $v = u - s = \begin{pmatrix}
  c_1x_w + s_1y_w -a_1\\
  -s_1x_w+c_1y_w \\
  z_w-d_1
\end{pmatrix}$. To make the math easier, we can also undo the shoulder tilt. Let 
$$\begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    Y \\
    Z
\end{pmatrix} = R_x\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)v = \begin{pmatrix}
  c_1x_w + s_1y_w -a_1\\
  -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s_1x_w+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}c_1y_w+\frac{1}{2}z_w-\frac{1}{2}d_1 \\
  \frac{1}{2}s_1x_w-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}c_1y_w+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}z_w-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}d_1
\end{pmatrix}$$

In the elbow aligned frame, the vector from the elbow to the wrist center is $b_{\text{aligned}} = \begin{pmatrix}
    d_4 \\
    0 \\
    d_3
\end{pmatrix}$

To express the same vector in the shoulder-aligned frame, we add the elbow rotation: $b = R_z(q_3)b_{\text{aligned}} = \begin{pmatrix}
    c_3d_4 \\
    s_3d_4 \\
    d_3
\end{pmatrix}$.

The vector between from the shoulder and the elbow in the shoulder-aligned frame is $a=\begin{pmatrix}
    a_2 \\
    d_2 \\
    0
\end{pmatrix}$

By additivity, we can now express the vector from the shoulder to the wrist center in the shoulder-aligned frame as $a + b = \begin{pmatrix}
    a_2+c_3d_4 \\
    d_2+s_3d_4 \\
    d_3
\end{pmatrix}$. To express it in the waist-aligned frame, we simply remove the shoulder angle $q_2$ by multiplying by $R_y(q_2)$ on the left. Note that is vector is also $[X, Y, Z]$:
$$ \begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    Y \\
    Z
\end{pmatrix} = R_y(q_2)(a+b)$$

We can sum the square components to obtain $N = X^2+Y^2+Z^2$:

$$N = (a_2+c_3d_4)^2 + (d_2+s_3d_4)^2 + (d_3)^2= a_2^2 + 2a_2d_4c_3 + d_4^2c_3^2 + d_2^2 + 2d_2d_4s_3 + d_4^2s_3^2 + d_3^2 =  a_2^2 + d_2^2 + d_3^2 + d_4^2 + 2a_2d_4c_3 + 2d_2d_4s_3$$

Knowing that $Y = d_2 +d_4s_3 \implies s_3 = \frac{Y-d_2}{d_4}$, we can replace $s_3$ in the previous expression and then isolate $c_3$:

$$N = a_2^2 + d_2^2 + d_3^2 + d_4^2 + 2a_2d_4c_3 + 2d_2d_4\frac{Y-d_2}{d_4} = a_2^2 + d_2^2 + d_3^2 + d_4^2 + 2a_2d_4c_3 + 2d_2Y-2d_2^2 $$

Let $K = d_2^2 - a_2^2 - d_3^2 - d_4^2$
$$ \implies c_3 = \frac{N + K -2d_2Y}{2a_2d_4}$$

and thus $q_3 = \text{atan2}(s_3,c_3)$.

To find q2:

$$\begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    Y \\
    Z
\end{pmatrix} = R_y(q_2) \begin{pmatrix}
    a_2 + c_3d_4 \\
    Y \\
    d_3
\end{pmatrix}$$

Let $A = a_2 + c_3d_4$.

$$\begin{pmatrix}
    X \\
    Y \\
    Z
\end{pmatrix} = R_y(q_2) \begin{pmatrix}
    c_2A+s_2d_3\\
    Y \\
    -s_2A+c_2d_3
\end{pmatrix}$$

Let $C_2 = AX+d_3Z = c_2(A^2 + d_3^2),\,S_2=d_3X-Az = s_2(d_3^2+A^2)$

then $q_2 = \text{atan2}(S_2,C_2)$

To solve q1 we will construct a degree-4 polynomial.

First note that $X^2+Y^2+Z^2 = ||R_x\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)v|| = ||v||$.

$$ \implies X^2+Y^2+Z^2 = (c_1x_w+s_1y_w-a_1)^2+(-s_1x_w+c_1y_w)^2+(z_w-d_1)^2$$

$$ = (c_1x_w+s_1y_w)^2 - 2c_1x_wa_1 - 2s_1y_wa_1 + a_1^2 + s_1^2x_w^2 - 2s_1c_1x_wy_ww + c_1^2y_w^2 + z_w^2 - 2z_wd_1 + d_1^2 $$
$$ = c_1^2x_w^2 + 2c_1s_1x_wy_w + s_1^2y_w^2 - 2c_1x_wa_1 - 2s_1y_wa_1 + a_1^2 + s_1^2x_w^2 - 2s_1c_1x_wy_ww + c_1^2y_w^2 + z_w^2 - 2z_wd_1 + d_1^2 $$
$$ = x_w^2 + y_w^2 - 2x_wc_1a_1 - 2y_ws_1a_1 + a_1^2 + z_w^2 -2z_wd_1 + d_1^2$$

which happens to be a linear function of $(c_1,s_1)$.

From the past expressions of $s_3$ and $c_3$ found when solving for $q_3$, and noting that $c_3^2 + s_3^2 = 1$, we have

$$4a_2^2(Y-d_2)^2 + (N + K - 2d_2Y)^2 = 4a_2^2d_4^2 $$

using the half angle formulas $$t=\tan(\frac{\theta}{2}),\,\cos(theta)=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\,\sin(theta)=\frac{2t}{1+t^2} $$
