\section{Jacobian and singularities}
\label{sec:jacobian}

In this section we construct the geometric Jacobian of CuRo6R from the PoE model.
The MATLAB PoE code keeps the intermediate transforms. These give each
joint's current unit direction $\vect{w}_i$ and a point $\vect{p}_i$ on its axis, both in
\texttt{base\_link}. The current axis direction $\vect{w}_i(\vect{q})$ is obtained by
rotating the home direction $\vect{\omega}_i$ through the preceding joint motions.
For a revolute joint, the Jacobian column is
\begin{equation}
 {}^0J_k(:,i)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\vect{w}_i\times(\vect{p}_k-\vect{p}_i)\\\vect{w}_i\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,6.
 \label{eq:jacobian-column}
\end{equation}
The upper rows give linear velocity and the lower rows give angular
velocity. We use $k=6$ for the endpoint. Choosing $k=5$ instead refers the
same tool motion to the wrist centre.

\textbf{Arm and wrist blocks.}
Here ${}^3J_5$ denotes this full Jacobian at the wrist centre, expressed
along the physical link-3 axes (\texttt{link\_3\_d3}). Both velocity blocks are rotated by
$({}^0R_3)^T$; the velocities remain relative to the base.
All three wrist axes pass through $\vect{p}_5$, so their linear columns vanish.
With $c_i=\cos q_i$ and $s_i=\sin q_i$, the result has the form
\begin{equation}
 {}^3J_5=\begin{pmatrix}J_{v,\mathrm{arm}}&0\\J_{\omega,\mathrm{arm}}&J_{\omega,\mathrm{wrist}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 J_{\omega,\mathrm{wrist}}=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&c_5\\
 0&-s_4&c_4s_5\\
 0&c_4&s_4s_5
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:jacobian-wrist}
\end{equation}
The subscripts $v$ and $\omega$ denote linear and angular velocity.
The $3\times3$ blocks $J_{v,\mathrm{arm}}$ and $J_{\omega,\mathrm{arm}}$
map the rates of joints 1--3 to these two velocities; $J_{\omega,\mathrm{wrist}}$
maps the rates of joints 4--6 to angular velocity. Their linear contribution
at the wrist centre is zero.

We obtain an exact symbolic expression of ${}^3J_5$ in MATLAB, from which the determinant is extracted.
The zero block of $J={}^3J_5$ makes the determinant easy to evaluate:
\begin{equation}
 \det J(\vect{q})=-\sin q_5\,\det J_{v,\mathrm{arm}}(q_2,q_3).
 \label{eq:jacobian-determinant}
\end{equation}
Using the DH lengths, define $U=a_2+d_4c_3$, $X=Uc_2+d_3s_2$, and
$Z=-Us_2+d_3c_2$. The explicit arm determinant is
\begin{equation}
 \det J_{v,\mathrm{arm}}=-\frac{d_4}{2}
 \left[a_1Zc_3-\sqrt3\,a_1Us_3
       -\sqrt3\,X(a_2s_3-d_2c_3)\right].
 \label{eq:jacobian-arm-factor}
\end{equation}
Changing the expression frame or velocity reference point preserves
$\det J$, provided all six columns describe the same full tool motion.
The wrist factor vanishes at $q_5=0$ or
$\pi$ modulo $2\pi$. Axes 4 and 6 then lie on the same line, so one
independent rotation is lost. The arm factor depends jointly on $q_2$ and
$q_3$. Thus the wrist and arm conditions separate, while the arm condition
must be studied in the $(q_2,q_3)$ plane, as shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:arm-singularity-map}.

A full configuration is regular exactly when both factors are nonzero.
Numerically, we monitor the smallest singular value of \(J\). It becomes 
zero at a singularity, and small values indicate that the Jacobian is 
close to losing rank.
